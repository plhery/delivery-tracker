A tracking status is the carrier's latest scan in a few words, and most ask nothing of you. "Label created" means the carrier doesn't have your parcel yet, "in transit" means it's somewhere between two scans, and "out for delivery" means it's on today's van. Only a few need you: a missed delivery, a parcel waiting for pickup, a customs charge and a "delivered" with nothing at the door.

## What are the stages of package tracking?

Every parcel passes the same stops, and one from abroad meets three more.

:::journey
- label | Label created | The carrier has the details, not the parcel.
- warehouse | In transit | Picked up, then hub to hub, with silence on the road.
- plane | Left the country | Abroad only. No scans in the air.
- customs | Customs | Abroad only. Sometimes a charge to pay.
- handover | Local carrier | Abroad only. Its scans may show only on its own site.
- truck | Out for delivery | On today's van.
- locker | Ready for pickup | Kept at a post office, shop or locker.
- home | Delivered | Handed over, or left where the carrier counts as yours.
:::

## What do USPS, Royal Mail and Australia Post statuses mean?

Same stages, different words:

| Stage | USPS | Royal Mail | Australia Post |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Label created | `Shipping Label Created, USPS Awaiting Item` | `We're expecting it` | `Processing` |
| Picked up | `USPS in Possession of Item` | `We've got it` | `We've got it` |
| In transit | `In Transit` | `On its way` | `It's on its way` |
| Out for delivery | `Out for Delivery` | `Ready for delivery` (still at the office) | `It's coming today` |
| Ready for pickup | `Available for Pickup` | `Ready for collection` | `Awaiting collection` |
| Delivered | `Delivered` | `Your item was delivered` | `Delivered` |

## "Label created" or "pre-shipment": does the carrier have it?

No: the seller has printed a label and sent the details, nothing more. USPS says the parcel "has not yet been given to USPS", and UPS's `Label Created` changes once UPS has it. An Post words it `Sender/retailer has shared details of your item to An Post`. Some US parcels show `Picked Up by Shipping Partner, USPS Awaiting Item`: a partner company has it and will hand it to USPS.

Australia Post's `Pending` (nothing scanned yet) should update within 24 to 48 hours. If nothing moves, ask the seller, not the carrier: USPS, An Post and Evri say so.

## What does "in transit" mean?

The carrier has your parcel, somewhere in its network. That doesn't mean it's moving: FedEx's `On the way` can mean in a vehicle or at a facility. `Arrived at` and `Departed` lines are hub scans, which USPS says can repeat from one facility on different days.

Between hubs, silence is normal:

- FedEx calls more than 24 hours without a scan "not unusual".
- UPS may not scan a long-distance parcel again before the destination hub.
- Royal Mail scans Signed For items only when it gets them and when it delivers.

### How long will a parcel be in transit?

Evri typically delivers 1 to 3 days after receiving a parcel, while Australia Post's `It's on its way` can last "a while" on long trips. Quiet past the carrier's own waiting time? See [why tracking stops updating](guide:tracking-not-updating).

## Parcels from abroad: left the country, customs, local carrier

- **Left the country.** It's booked onto a flight, ship or train. For a parcel heading to Germany, DHL warns the next update can be several days away. Royal Mail's `OE` (Office of Exchange) means it reached import customs. On an item from abroad, Australia Post's `It's on its way` means it's in Australia.
- **Customs.** `In customs` (Royal Mail) or `Item held by customs` (Canada Post) is usually routine: Canada Post calls it standard for every international parcel, screened by the Canada Border Services Agency, and can't step in. DHL speaks of several business days; none of these carriers gives a general maximum. Once released, Canada Post's tracking says it has cleared customs. Act only on a request, such as Royal Mail's `Fee to pay`; see [parcels held at customs](guide:customs).
- **Local carrier.** UPS's `Transferred to Post Office for Delivery` means the post office does the last leg: allow an extra day or two. DHL points you to the destination carrier's tracking. Ordered from AliExpress, Temu or Shein? See [tracking a parcel from China](guide:tracking-from-china).

## Does "out for delivery" mean it arrives today?

Usually, yes:

- **USPS:** `Out for Delivery` means today, perhaps at a different time from your usual mail. No delivery recorded by 9 pm? It becomes `Awaiting Delivery Scan` (or `Delivery Status Not Updated`), and USPS expects it the next business day.
- **UPS:** typically 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. for homes.
- **FedEx:** generally 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.
- **Australia Post:** `It's coming today` once the postie has loaded it, and it may text you a 2-hour window.

Two look-alikes: Royal Mail's `Ready for delivery` means it has reached your local delivery office, not the van. And at DHL in Germany, a loading scan shown more than once means the round wasn't finished: a delay of 1 to 2 days is possible.

[Peek](/) checks up to every 2 minutes once a parcel is out for delivery, and its notifications can be set to delivery day only.

## "Delivery attempted" or "notice left": what now?

This one needs you: nobody could take the parcel, or the driver couldn't get in.

:::steps
- Read the notice | Card, text or email: where the parcel went, and a number to quote.
- Check the deadline | From 2 days to 3 weeks, counted in calendar, working or business days.
- Rebook or redirect | On the carrier's own site or app: another day, a neighbour, a pickup point.
- Collect with ID | Bring photo ID and the card. Someone collecting for you may need your signed note and their own ID.
:::

| Carrier | After a missed delivery | Kept for pickup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| USPS (US) | `Notice Left`: no automatic second try; book a redelivery or collect | Signature mail "usually 15 days"; Priority Mail Express 5 calendar days |
| UPS (US) | Up to three attempts, or a UPS Access Point | 7 calendar days at an Access Point |
| FedEx (US) | Next-day reattempt, or a nearby FedEx location | 7 days |
| Royal Mail (UK) | Another try the next working day, then a 'Something for you' card | 18 days, 21 with a customs charge |
| Evri (UK) | Three attempts and a calling card; no depot collection | If sent to a ParcelShop, 10 days; a locker, 3 |
| An Post (IE) | At least one attempt, then a "sorry we missed you" notice | 5 working days, 16 from abroad |
| Canada Post | `Notice left`: a card naming the post office | 15 calendar days |
| Australia Post | A card or message; a Post Office, or maybe a Parcel Locker if your mobile number is on the item | Locker 2 business days; returned after 10 business days |

> A text saying your delivery "has been stopped" and asking you to sort it out online is a scam, says An Post, which never sends payment links by email or text. Rebook only on the carrier's own site or app.

## "Ready for pickup" or "awaiting collection": where is it?

It's at a post office, shop or locker, until the deadline in the table above. Canada Post, like USPS, says `Available for Pickup`; UPS says `Awaiting Customer Pickup`, and `Delivered to a UPS Access Point` means it's ready for you there.

Wait for this status or the carrier's message before going: USPS makes items available from the next business day, and Australia Post's notification gives the date and time. Canada Post prints the pickup place on the card, and it can change from one delivery to the next.

## Does "delivered" mean it's in your hands?

Not always. It means the driver scanned it as handed over or left:

- **With a neighbour.** Royal Mail's `Your item was delivered` covers one, named in the message, with a card left.
- **Somewhere safe.** Without a signature, UPS picks a spot out of sight and weather: front porch, side door, back porch, garage. USPS adds the mailbox, back entrance and others at home; a key in your mail compartment means a parcel locker. Canada Post's `Delivered as a safe drop` usually comes with a photo.

Australia Post also shows `Delivered` once you've collected it from a Post Office or Parcel Locker. Nothing there? Check the full tracking for a photo, a name or a place, then see [delivered but not received](guide:delivered-not-received).

## Does a delivery exception mean it's lost?

No. FedEx says a `Delivery exception` "doesn't necessarily mean" the parcel will be late, and asks nothing of you; its examples include a customs delay and "recipient wasn't home". UPS's `Exception` means something unexpected may move the delivery date, with the reason under Shipment Progress.

FedEx's `Running late` means it will likely miss its estimated date. As for how long, Australia Post says most items flagged `Delayed` arrive within 5 business days of the expected date.

Look closer when the reason is an address or a payment: Canada Post's `Verifying recipient's address; Possible delay` ends in a return if the address can't be confirmed. Royal Mail's `Internal exception occurred` is a website error, not your parcel.

## Why was my parcel returned to sender?

The carriers' reasons: not collected in time, a wrong or incomplete address, a refusal, unpaid customs charges, damage. Some returns don't say so:

- **USPS** `Insufficient Address`: it's already heading back, and the sender must post it again with new postage.
- **Australia Post** `Contact sender`: a return, after wrong details or 10 business days uncollected.
- **Royal Mail** `Forwarded to Returns Centre`: with no return address on it, the centre opens it to find one.

Don't count on stopping it: "Once the return process starts, we can't stop it," says Canada Post. Ask the seller to send it again or refund you.
